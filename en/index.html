Does the displayed purchase cost include deposits and withdrawals?

No. The cost ranking compares the purchase debit; cashback mode subtracts the included cashback. I show deposit, withdrawal and conversion fees separately within each card; network costs depend on the network you choose. The total cost depends on the route and transfer amount.

Is the included cashback already available to spend?

Not always. Some cashback is displayed after the purchase, but its payout is still unconfirmed. I value credited Bybit points at the documented redemption ratio without a verified redemption, and BTC at a saved fixed valuation. Each card calculation states the basis for its amount.

Making the figures verifiable

How I calculate it

The test is one 158 ₴ purchase on 09.10.2026. I assume 1 USDT ≈ 1 USDC ≈ $1 for comparison; card denomination, spending asset and internal settlement currency may differ.

  • Debit: before balance minus after balance. Exceptions are USDT spent on the completed OKX screen, Bybit’s USDT debit in asset history, Trustee’s exact receipt and Solid’s Available to spend. Initial balances are kept separate from completed payments.
  • The NBU rate on the test date was 44.8526 ₴/$, giving a $3.522649746… purchase equivalent. Deviation includes visible costs, not only FX spread. I do not add included fees twice.
  • I deduct displayed Solid $0.11 and COCA $0.03 before credit, Bybit’s 36 credited points at 0.002 USDT/point = 0.072 USDT under redemption terms, and Kolo / WhiteBIT BTC at a fixed valuation. Bybit point redemption into USDT is unverified. Trustee Points contribute $0 to the current cash calculation.
Cashback rules, models and precision

Potential assumes $1 000 of eligible purchases over a full month on the base or next tier, after the cap and annual fee / 12. It excludes FX, funding, categories, APY and token capital costs. COCA’s cap limits claiming rather than accrual. This is a terms-based model, not measured monthly profit.

I value displayed Kolo BTC and credited WhiteBIT BTC at a fixed $82976.035/BTC quote. This is neither spendable USD nor a measured sale rate; each card’s details contain the valuation and source.

One small purchase and rounded balances limit ranking precision. Sub-cent differences may depend on rounding; fixed fees change results at larger amounts. An app status does not always prove card-network settlement.

Methodology ↗

How I calculate Earning and total benefit

With even spending, average balance = ($1,000 + $0) / 2 = $500. APR: $500 × rate × 30 / 365. For APY, I use the equivalent daily rate: $500 × 30 × ((1 + APY)^(1/365) − 1). The model does not reinvest income. If the source does not specify APR/APY, I show both estimates as a range of assumptions, not a guaranteed payout.

I assume a constant rate and eligibility of the entire average balance. Subscriptions paid early, deposits in installments, and time outside an eligible balance change the result. Estimates exclude taxes and unknown claim costs; each card lists its program’s limits.

I calculate total benefit as cashback + spendable income − FX, conversion, funding and tier fees. I subtract each tier fee once. Balance income is not attributed to the UAH 158 purchase and does not change its ranking. In this model, 5% a year covers only about 0.20% of the monthly budget; debit cost and cashback remain the main factors for UAH spending.
